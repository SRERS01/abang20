## Part A: Recovery Sites & Environmental Considerations

### 1. Hot Sites, 2. Warm Sites & 3. Cold Sites
* **Concept:** Alternate off-site facilities prepared to resume business operations if the primary site is destroyed or offline:

| Site Type | Infrastructure & Data Readiness | Activation Time (RTO) | Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Hot Site** | Fully equipped replica of the primary data center with live hardware, network links, and **real-time synchronous data replication**. | **Minutes to Hours** (Near-zero) | **Highest** |
| **2. Warm Site** | Pre-installed servers, racks, HVAC, and network connectivity, **but** lacks live production data (requires restoring recent backups before cutover). | **Hours to Days** | **Moderate** |
| **3. Cold Site** | Empty physical building with power, cooling, and raised floors, **but zero IT hardware** installed (servers must be purchased/shipped and configured from scratch). | **Weeks to Months** | **Lowest** |

## Part C: Power & Storage Redundancy

### 13. UPS, 14. Redundant Power Supplies, 15. Generators & 16. Surge Protectors
* **Concept:** Layered electrical protection against blackouts, brownouts, and voltage spikes:
  * **Surge Protectors:** Absorb sudden high-voltage spikes/transients to prevent hardware fry.
  * **UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply):** Battery backup providing **immediate, zero-millisecond bridge power** during utility outages so servers stay online until generators start or shut down gracefully.
  * **Generators:** Diesel, natural gas, or hybrid fuel generators providing **long-term extended power** (days/weeks) during prolonged utility grid failures.
  * **Redundant Power Supplies (Dual PSUs):** Servers equipped with two independent PSUs plugged into separate electrical circuits/PDUs (`Feed A` and `Feed B`) so a single PSU or breaker failure causes zero downtime.

### 17. Storage Redundancy (RAID Levels)
* **Concept:** Combining multiple physical disks into a **RAID (Redundant Array of Independent Disks)** to protect against drive failure *(Note: RAID provides **Availability**, NOT a backup against ransomware or accidental deletion!)*:

| RAID Level | Mechanism | Min Drives | Fault Tolerance |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **RAID 0** | Striping (performance only, zero redundancy) | 2 | **0 drives** (1 drive loss = total data loss) |
| **RAID 1** | Mirroring (exact 1:1 copy across two drives) | 2 | **1 drive** |
| **RAID 5** | Striping with distributed single parity | 3 | **1 drive** |
| **RAID 6** | Striping with distributed dual parity | 4 | **2 drives** |
| **RAID 10 (1+0)** | Striped mirrors (high speed + high redundancy) | 4 | **1 drive per mirrored pair** |

## Part D: Backups & Disaster Recovery Testing

### 18. Backup Strategies, 19. Backup Retention, 20. Immutable Backups & 21. Backup Scope
* **Concept:**
  * **Backup Strategies:**
    * **Full Backup:** Copies all selected data every run (fastest restore, slowest backup, most storage).
    * **Incremental Backup:** Copies only files changed since the **last backup of any type** (fastest backup, smallest storage, slowest restore—requires Full + every subsequent Incremental).
    * **Differential Backup:** Copies all files changed since the **last Full backup** (moderate backup speed, faster restore—requires only Full + latest Differential).
  * **3-2-1 Backup Rule:** **3** copies of data, on **2** different media types, with **1** copy stored off-site/offline.
  * **Backup Retention:** Governance policy defining how long daily, weekly, monthly, and yearly backups are kept before secure deletion.
  * **Immutable Backups:** Write-Once-Read-Many (**WORM**) backups (e.g., AWS S3 Object Lock, air-gapped tapes) that **cannot be altered or deleted—even by root/admin accounts—until the retention timer expires**, defeating ransomware gangs that target backups first.
  * **Backup Scope:** Defining exactly which critical databases, configurations, VMs, and user directories are included vs. excluded (e.g., excluding temp/cache files).
* **Practical Task (Create a Timestamped Backup & Apply Linux Immutability Attribute):**

### 22. Restoration Testing, 23. Failover Testing, 24. Simulation & 25. Parallel Processing
* **Concept:** Validating recovery plans *before* a real emergency strikes:
  * **Restoration Testing:** Periodically extracting backup archives into an isolated sandbox and verifying checksums and database integrity ("An untested backup is not a backup").
  * **Failover Testing:** Intentionally disconnecting a primary server/link during a maintenance window to confirm traffic automatically switches to the secondary node/site.
  * **Simulation (Tabletop / Walk-Through Exercises):** Scenario-based walkthroughs where incident response and leadership teams practice decision-making against simulated ransomware or data-center outages without disrupting production.
  * **Parallel Processing:** Running the primary production system and the recovery system **simultaneously** side-by-side, comparing outputs to verify the recovery environment handles full workload accurately before cutting over completely.

### 26. Disaster Recovery (DR) vs. 27. Business Continuity (BCP)
* **Concept:**

### 29. RTO, 30. RPO, 31. MTTR & 32. MTBF (Recovery Metrics)
* **Concept:** Quantitative benchmarks established during a **Business Impact Analysis (BIA)** to govern SLA design, backup schedules, and hardware reliability:

| Metric | Full Name | Core Definition | Practical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **29. RTO** | **Recovery Time Objective** | Maximum tolerable **downtime** allowed to restore a system/service after an outage occurs (**Forward-looking target**). | `RTO = 2 hours` → Web portal must be back online within 2 hours of a crash. |
| **30. RPO** | **Recovery Point Objective** | Maximum tolerable **data loss** measured in time—dictates backup/replication frequency (**Backward-looking target**). | `RPO = 15 mins` → Snapshots/replication must run at least every 15 minutes. |
| **31. MTTR** | **Mean Time to Repair / Recover** | Average actual elapsed time required to diagnose, repair, and restore a failed component to normal operation. *(Must be ≤ RTO)*. | `MTTR = 45 mins` → On average, technicians fix failed switches in 45 minutes. |
| **32. MTBF** | **Mean Time Between Failures** | Average operational uptime a repairable system/component runs **before** experiencing a failure (measures hardware/system reliability). | `MTBF = 4,380 hours` → Server runs ~6 months on average between hardware faults. |

* **Practical Task (Build a BIA SLA & Reliability Calculator in Python):**
