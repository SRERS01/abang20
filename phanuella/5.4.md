## Part A: Compliance Governance, Financial Integrity & Monitoring (8 Items)

### 1. Compliance Training & 2. Data Handling
* **Concept:**
  * **Compliance Training:** Role-specific mandatory education ensuring employees understand regulatory duties (GDPR, HIPAA, PCI-DSS, AML) and proper security behaviors.
  * **Data Handling:** Enforcing strict lifecycle rules for collecting, labeling, encrypting, transmitting, storing, and disposing of regulated data based on classification.

### 3. Anti-Money Laundering (AML), 4. Counter-Terrorism Financing (CTF) & 5. Anti-Bribery
* **Concept:** Financial integrity laws requiring organizations (especially banks, fintechs, mobile money operators, and international enterprises) to monitor transactions, verify customer identities (**KYC — Know Your Customer**), block sanctioned entities, and report suspicious activity:
  * **AML (Anti-Money Laundering):** Controls detecting and preventing criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income (e.g., structuring/smurfing deposits just below reporting thresholds).
  * **CTF (Counter-Terrorism Financing):** Controls blocking financial transfers to terrorist organizations or sanctioned individuals/jurisdictions.
  * **Anti-Bribery (e.g., FCPA, UK Bribery Act):** Prohibits offering or accepting improper payments, kickbacks, or gifts to influence government officials or business contracts.
* **Practical Task (Build an Automated AML/CTF Transaction Structuring Detector in Python):**
  ```python
  # Detect "Structuring / Smurfing" (multiple rapid transfers just below a 5,000,000 XAF reporting threshold)
  transactions = [
      {"user": "acct_882", "amount_xaf": 4_900_000, "dest_country": "CM"},
      {"user": "acct_882", "amount_xaf": 4_850_000, "dest_country": "CM"},
      {"user": "acct_882", "amount_xaf": 4_950_000, "dest_country": "CM"}
  ]

  threshold = 5_000_000
  near_threshold = [tx for tx in transactions if 0.9 * threshold <= tx["amount_xaf"] < threshold]
  if len(near_threshold) >= 3:
      print(f"[AML/CTF ALERT] Potential transaction structuring detected for {near_threshold['user']} ({len(near_threshold)} transfers near threshold)!")
  ```

### 6. Compliance Monitoring, 7. Attestations & 8. Acknowledgements
* **Concept:**
  * **Compliance Monitoring:** Continuous automated auditing (CSPM, SIEM compliance dashboards, DLP alerts, SCAP scans) verifying ongoing adherence to regulatory standards.
  * **Attestations:** Formal written statements or third-party audit certificates (**PCI-DSS AoC**, **SOC 2**, manager quarterly access re-certifications) confirming that required controls are actively operating.
  * **Acknowledgements:** Signed employee records (digital or physical) proving staff received, read, and agreed to abide by policies (**AUP**, **BYOD**, **NDA**, **Code of Ethics**)—essential for HR/legal accountability.

## Part C: Data Privacy & Data Subject Rights (8 Concepts)

### Privacy Rights (GDPR / CCPA / Data Protection Laws):
* **1. Right to be Forgotten (Right to Erasure):** Individuals can request permanent deletion of their personal data when it is no longer necessary for the purpose collected or consent is withdrawn *(unless overridden by legal retention or a Litigation Hold)*.
* **2. Opt-In and Opt-Out:**
  * **Opt-In:** Requires **explicit, affirmative user consent** *before* collecting/processing data or sending marketing (mandatory under GDPR).
  * **Opt-Out:** Data collection/sharing occurs by default unless the user actively unsubscribes or clicks *"Do Not Sell My Personal Information"* (common under CCPA).
* **3. Data Correction (Right to Rectification):** Right of individuals to have inaccurate or incomplete personal records updated promptly.
* **4. Processing Restrictions & 5. Processing Prevention:** Right to temporarily freeze/limit how personal data is used (e.g., while accuracy is contested) or object entirely to automated profiling/direct marketing.

### Privacy Governance Roles:
* **6. Data Controller:** Organization that determines the **purposes (`why`) and means (`how`)** of processing personal data.
* **7. Data Processor:** Third-party entity that processes personal data strictly **on behalf of and under the instructions of the Data Controller** (e.g., cloud hosting provider, payroll service).
* **8. Data Ownership:** Legal and executive accountability over datasets—assigning classification labels, approving access, and ensuring privacy compliance.

* **Practical Task (Implement a Privacy Subject Rights Processor [Erasure vs. Litigation Hold] in Python):**
  ```python
  user_db = {
      "USR-101": {"name": "Ambe Fon",   "consent_opt_in": True,  "legal_hold": False},
      "USR-102": {"name": "Grace Ngwa", "consent_opt_in": False, "legal_hold": True}
  }

  def process_erasure_request(user_id):
      record = user_db.get(user_id)
      if not record:
          return "User not found."
      if record["legal_hold"]:
          return f"[DENIED - LEGAL HOLD] Cannot erase '{record['name']}': Active litigation hold overrides Right to be Forgotten!"
      del user_db[user_id]
      return f"[ERASED] Personal data for '{record['name']}' permanently deleted per Right to be Forgotten."

  print(process_erasure_request("USR-101"))
  print(process_erasure_request("USR-102"))
  ```

## Part D: Legal Compliance (3 Mandates)

### 1. Legal Holds (Litigation Holds)
* **Concept:** Formal directive issued by **Legal Counsel** requiring the organization to preserve all electronic and physical records (emails, chat logs, database rows, backups) relevant to pending or anticipated litigation, audits, or regulatory investigations. **Immediately suspends automated data-retention deletion and Right-to-be-Forgotten erasure.**

### 2. Legal Orders
* **Concept:** Binding judicial or governmental directives—such as **Subpoenas**, **Search Warrants**, **Court Orders**, or **National Security Letters**—compelling an organization to produce specific records or preserve evidence under strict chain-of-custody rules.

### 3. Data Retention Requirements
* **Concept:** Statutory and regulatory mandates specifying the **minimum** time records must be kept for audits/investigations (e.g., 5–7 years for financial/tax/AML records, 6 years for HIPAA documentation) and the **maximum** time personal data may be kept before mandatory disposal (GDPR storage limitation principle).
