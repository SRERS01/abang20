## Part A: Audit Evidence Gathering & Threat Frameworks (10 Items)

### 1. Data Gathering, 2. Sampling, 3. Questionnaires, 4. Surveys, 5. Interviews & 6. Assertions
* **Concept:** Techniques auditors use to collect and validate compliance evidence:
  * **Data Gathering:** Collecting system logs, firewall configs, access lists, training records, and policy documents.
  * **Sampling:** Selecting a statistically representative subset of records (e.g., auditing 30 random employee terminations out of 1,000) when testing 100% of records is impractical.
  * **Questionnaires & 4. Surveys:** Written yes/no or scored forms distributed to vendors or departments to gather standardized control self-reports.
  * **Interviews:** Direct one-on-one discussions with admins, HR, and managers to verify whether actual daily practices match written SOPs.
  * **Assertions:** Formal management representations/claims (e.g., *"All production databases are encrypted at rest"*) that the auditor must independently verify with technical evidence.
* **Practical Task (Perform Statistical Audit Sampling on Termination Logs in Python):**
  ```python
  import random

  # Randomly sample 3 terminated employee records from a population for deprovisioning audit
  terminated_users = [f"EMP-{i:04d}" for i in range(1, 51)]
  random.seed(42)
  audit_sample = random.sample(terminated_users, k=3)
  print("Selected Audit Sample for Access Revocation Check:", audit_sample)
  ```

### 7. Reference Sources, 8. MITRE ATT&CK, 9. Cyber Kill Chain & 10. Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis
* **Concept:** Authoritative threat-modeling and assessment frameworks used to benchmark detection coverage:

| Framework / Reference | Core Structure & Security+ Exam Role |
| :--- | :--- |
| **7. Reference Sources** | Authoritative advisories, vendor hardening guides, CVE/NVD databases, and threat intelligence feeds used to validate baselines. |

| **10. Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis** | 4-vertex relational graph linking **Adversary**, **Capability**, **Infrastructure**, and **Victim** (connected by socio-political and technology meta-features) for incident attribution and pivoting. |

## Part B: Audit Governance, Internal & External Audits (12 Items)

### 11. Audit Charters, 12. Audit Frequency & 13. Gap Analysis
* **Concept:**
  * **Audit Charter:** Formal document approved by the Board/Audit Committee defining the internal audit function's purpose, authority, independence, and unrestricted access to records/systems.
  * **Audit Frequency:** Scheduled cadence of audits (annual, semi-annual, quarterly, or event-driven after major mergers/breaches) dictated by risk level and regulatory mandates.
  * **Gap Analysis:** Comparing an organization's **current security posture** against a **target framework/standard** (e.g., ISO 27001 or NIST CSF) to identify missing controls and build a remediation roadmap.

### Internal vs. External Audit & Assessment Types:

| Audit / Assessment Type | Scope, Independence & Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |
| **14. Internal Audits** | Conducted by internal auditors reporting to the Audit Committee to evaluate internal controls and operational efficiency. |
| **15. Compliance Audits** | Focused verification measuring strict pass/fail adherence to specific laws, regulations, or policies (PCI-DSS, HIPAA, GDPR). |
| **16. Audit Committees** | Independent subcommittee of the **Board of Directors** overseeing audit independence, financial/cyber risk, and remediation tracking. |
| **17. Self-Assessments** | Internal control checklists completed by system owners/teams to spot weaknesses ahead of formal audits. |
| **18. External Examinations** | Formal supervisory inspections conducted by government or financial regulators (e.g., central banking authorities). |
| **19. External Assessments** | Technical evaluations (risk assessments, architecture reviews, pen tests) performed by hired external security consultants. |
| **20. Regulatory Assessments** | Mandatory compliance evaluations required by government statutes or sector regulators. |
| **21. Independent Third-Party Audits** | Unbiased formal certification/attestation audits performed by accredited external firms (**SOC 2 Type II**, **ISO 27001**). |
| **22. Benchmarking** | Comparing an organization's security metrics, maturity scores, or configurations against industry peers or **CIS Benchmarks**. |

* **Practical Task (Run a NIST/ISO Control Gap Analysis Calculator in Python):**

## Part C: Penetration Testing & Reconnaissance (9 Items)

### Environment Knowledge Levels (3 Models):
* **23. Known Environment *(White Box)*:** Tester receives full architecture diagrams, source code, IP lists, and admin credentials (fastest, deepest flaw discovery).
* **24. Unknown Environment *(Black Box)*:** Tester receives zero internal information—only the company name/domain (most realistic simulation of an external attacker).
* **25. Partially Known Environment *(Gray Box)*:** Tester receives limited insider context such as standard user credentials or partial network documentation.

### Testing Perspectives & Reconnaissance (6 Techniques):
* **26. Physical Penetration Testing:** Attempting physical entry (tailgating, badge cloning, lock picking, planting rogue drop-boxes) under signed Rules of Engagement.
* **27. Offensive Testing *(Red Team)*:** Adversary emulation attempting stealthy end-to-end compromise without alerting defenders.
* **28. Defensive Testing *(Blue Team)*:** Validating SIEM alerts, EDR telemetry, firewall blocks, and SOC incident response runbooks against simulated attacks.
* **29. Integrated Testing *(Purple Team)*:** Collaborative real-time exercise pairing Red and Blue teams together to execute attacks, verify detection immediately, and tune rules on the spot.
* **30. Passive Reconnaissance:** Collecting OSINT (`WHOIS`, DNS records, Shodan, Certificate Transparency logs, job postings) **without sending packets to target systems**.
* **31. Active Reconnaissance:** Directly probing target hosts over the network (`nmap` port/version scans, banner grabbing, web crawling)—generates firewall/IDS logs.

* **Practical Task (Passive DNS/CT Recon vs. Authorized Localhost Active Scan):**
  ```bash
  # Passive Recon (Never touches target servers)
  nslookup -type=NS example.com

  # Active Recon (Strictly restricted to your own 127.0.0.1 loopback interface)
  nmap -sT -sV -p 22,80,443 127.0.0.1
  ```

### 32. Industry Standards, 33. International Standards & 34. Regional Standards
* **Concept:**
  * **Industry Standards:** Sector-specific security requirements governed by industry bodies—e.g., **PCI-DSS** (Payment Card Industry), **NERC CIP** (North American electric grid), **CIS Benchmarks**.

### 35. Functional Testing vs. 36. Behavioral Testing
* **Concept:** Two complementary approaches used during security audits, software QA, and control validation:
  * **35. Functional Testing:** Verifying that a security control, firewall rule, encryption module, or application feature **works strictly according to its defined design specifications** under expected inputs (Pass/Fail verification of *what* the control does).
    * *Examples:* Confirming a firewall ACL drops `TCP/23` (Telnet), verifying an account locks out after 5 failed password attempts, or checking that `TLS 1.1` connections are rejected.
  * **36. Behavioral Testing:** Observing and measuring how a system, application, network, or human workforce **behaves dynamically under realistic adversarial conditions, stress, or unexpected anomalies** (*how* the system or user reacts in practice).
    * *Examples:* Launching an unannounced **phishing simulation** to measure employee click/reporting behavior, executing a **purple-team lateral movement simulation** to verify EDR behavioral alerts trigger, or fuzzing an API with malformed payloads to observe error handling under stress.

* **Practical Task (Execute Both a Functional Security Control Test & a Behavioral Anomaly Simulation in Python):**
  ```python
  # 1. FUNCTIONAL TEST: Verify account lockout rule triggers at exactly 5 failed attempts
  def check_account_lockout(failed_attempts, threshold=5):
      return "LOCKED" if failed_attempts >= threshold else "ACTIVE"

  assert check_account_lockout(4) == "ACTIVE", "Functional Test 1 Failed"
  assert check_account_lockout(5) == "LOCKED", "Functional Test 2 Failed"
  print("[PASS - FUNCTIONAL TEST] Account lockout triggers strictly at threshold = 5.")

  # 2. BEHAVIORAL TEST: Measure employee phishing simulation behavior & EDR reaction
  phishing_sim_results = [
      {"employee": "alice", "action": "REPORTED_TO_SOC"},
      {"employee": "bob",   "action": "CLICKED_LINK"},
      {"employee": "ambe",  "action": "REPORTED_TO_SOC"}
  ]

  click_rate = (sum(1 for r in phishing_sim_results if r["action"] == "CLICKED_LINK") / len(phishing_sim_results)) * 100
  report_rate = (sum(1 for r in phishing_sim_results if r["action"] == "REPORTED_TO_SOC") / len(phishing_sim_results)) * 100
  print(f"[PASS - BEHAVIORAL TEST] Workforce Phishing Click Rate: {click_rate:.1f}% | SOC Report Rate: {report_rate:.1f}%")
  ```
