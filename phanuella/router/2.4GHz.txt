### Notes on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi: Enabled vs Disabled

2.4 GHz is a Wi-Fi frequency band that routers use to transmit wireless signals between the router and connected devices.

### 1\. 2.4 GHz ENABLED

Enabled = ON

This means your router broadcasts a Wi-Fi signal on the 2.4 GHz frequency.

Characteristics:

* Covers a relatively longer distance.

* Passes through walls and obstacles better than 5 GHz.

* Supports many older phones, laptops and smart devices.

* Usually offers lower speeds than 5 GHz.

* Can experience interference from Bluetooth devices and other electronics.

### 2\. 2.4 GHz DISABLED

Disabled = OFF

This means your router stops broadcasting Wi-Fi on the 2.4 GHz frequency.

Consequences:

* Devices that support only 2.4 GHz cannot connect through Wi-Fi.

* Devices that support 5 GHz can still connect if that band is enabled.

* Your Wi-Fi coverage may become shorter.

* You may experience less interference from some neighbouring networks.

* It does not switch off your entire Wi-Fi network.

### 3\. Practical comparison

| Feature          | 2.4 GHz ON             | 2.4 GHz OFF             |
| ---------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Wi-Fi coverage   | Longer                 | Usually shorter overall |
| Wall penetration | Better                 | 5 GHz is weaker         |
| Speed            | Generally lower        | Generally higher        |
| Older devices    | Supported              | May not connect         |
| 5 GHz devices    | Supported if available | Still supported         |
| Interference     | Often more             | Often less              |

### 4\. Which should you choose?

For your objective of limiting Wi-Fi access to your room, you can try:

2.4 GHz

OFF

5 GHz

ON

WPS

OFF

WPA2-AES / WPA3

ON

Important: Disabling 2.4 GHz does not guarantee that people outside your room cannot connect. Use a device whitelist and reduce transmission power if supported.

Also, check that your phone supports 5 GHz before disabling 2.4 GHz.
